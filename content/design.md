Most bitcoin wallets look like fintech utilities. Arké doesn't.

---

## The thesis

Beauty and self-custody are not in conflict.

The bitcoin community has, for good reasons, put trustlessness ahead of aesthetics. The result is software that works but rarely delights. Arké is a bet that this tradeoff isn't real, and that a wallet can be beautiful, private, and fully yours at once.

If that sounds obvious, look at what's out there. It isn't obvious yet.

---

## Built for one person

Arké's early users are bitcoiners, people deep in the ecosystem who are helping test the protocol. But the product isn't designed for them.

Every decision is made for someone who hasn't shown up yet. They're curious about bitcoin, put off by everything they've seen so far, and have no interest in reading a protocol explainer before sending money to a friend. If the app works for that person, it works for everyone. It doesn't work the other way around.

As the technology matures, the audience will widen. The design won't need to change, because it was never built for insiders.

---

## What Apple got right

Arké is built for iPhone, and not only for market reasons. Apple's design culture insists on craft. It's willing to take things away instead of adding them, and it treats how something feels as seriously as what it does. In consumer software, that's the closest match to what bitcoin stands for at the protocol level.

Both start from the idea that you shouldn't have to take the makers at their word. Apple earns trust through quality. Bitcoin earns it through cryptography. Arké tries to do both.

The goal is for Arké to feel at home on the iPhone. It doesn't copy Apple's look, but it's made with the same care.

## Visual language

Fortnite collaborates with Balenciaga. Travis Scott plays a concert inside a video game. Memes borrow from fashion shoots, and AI dreams up outfits that don't exist yet.

The lines between gaming, fashion, music, and internet culture have mostly dissolved, and so has the idea that a visual identity has to belong to one of those worlds. You take from wherever makes sense.

That's where Arké's imagery lives. It pulls from fashion editorials, AI generation, street culture, and surrealism. The point isn't luxury or aspiration. It's simply how visual culture works now. The look gives Arké a personality rather than a market position.

---

## Open source design

The design system, visual assets, and design decisions are open source along with the code. Other wallet builders are welcome to take whatever is useful, whether that's components, patterns, or the reasoning behind them.

One of Arké's goals is to widen what people think bitcoin software can look like. That only works if the work is out in the open for others to use.

---

## The Bitcoin Design Guide

Arké is a working implementation of the [Bitcoin Design Guide](https://bitcoin.design/guide/). The guide collects best practices for bitcoin UX: what good onboarding looks like, how to handle keys, how to explain fees and confirmation times without confusing people, and a lot more.

Arké treats the guide as the starting point. Following best practices is the baseline, and the design ambition builds on top of it.
